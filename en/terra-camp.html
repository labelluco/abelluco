Terra Camp: land measuring

Terra Camp measures a plot of land on the ground with your phone's GPS and compares it with the Spanish Cadastre: you know at once whether the boundaries you see in the field match the map.

For farmers, landowners, surveyors and anyone who wants to know how big their land really is.

Open the app All the apps

Works in your phone or computer browser, with nothing to install. Coming soon to Google Play.

What you can do

  • Walk and measure

    • Go to each corner and tap "Take vertex": the app takes several GPS readings and averages them, discarding the worst ones
    • On Android, keep measuring with the screen off, with a notification while it lasts
    • The app offers to close the polygon when you get back to the first vertex
    • If the app closes, the measurement continues where you left it

  • Compare with the Cadastre

    • The parcel where you stand, by cadastral reference, by polygon and parcel number, or by tapping it on the map
    • Overlap, measured and cadastral area, and the difference in m² and %
    • The areas that are only in one or the other, shown on the map
    • How many metres each vertex is from the boundary
    • A 3D view of the terrain with the parcel and the measured polygon

  • With the Pro version

    • Unlimited saved measurements
    • Configurable averaging: readings, seconds between them and how many are discarded
    • The estimated error of each vertex, and re-measuring one
    • Export to GeoJSON, KML, GPX and CSV
    • PDF report with the comparison, a scale drawing and the vertex table

  • Private

    • No accounts, no ads and no analytics
    • Measurements are stored only on your device

Free, with a one-time Pro purchase

Measuring, comparing with the Cadastre and the 3D view are free, with one saved measurement. The Pro version is a one-time purchase, with no subscription.

Accuracy depends on the phone's GPS and the surroundings: measurements are approximate and not valid for official boundary procedures, which require a surveyor.

Data sources

  • Cadastre: Spain's Directorate General for the Cadastre
  • Terrain model and PNOA aerial imagery: National Geographic Institute of Spain (IGN)
